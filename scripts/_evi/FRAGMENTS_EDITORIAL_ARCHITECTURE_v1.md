## 1. Executive thesis

Fragments is a documentary evidence chapter: one page that makes a stranger understand, within seconds, that DJ Lethal Skillz has been a participant inside hip-hop and turntablism culture for over three decades. The chapter is built on photographs of Skillz with the people who define the discipline — Grandmaster Flash, Q-Bert, Jazzy Jeff, Tony Touch, Apollo, Vinroc, Rob Swift, the Wu-Tang DJ, the Bomb Squad producer, the French and Japanese scenes — plus the places, events and documents of a career that spans Beirut, Dubai, London, KL, Phnom Penh, Japan, France and the United States. The section must read as evidence, not self-promotion. Names are presented museum-style so the visitor always knows WHO they are seeing. The archive orbits Skillz; the photograph of Skillz with Grandmaster Flash is the anchor of that orbit.

## 2. What Fragments is

- An archive being revealed: curated, edited, sequenced — not a dump.
- A relationships chapter: documented encounters with the culture's significant figures (the strongest surviving material, ~24 named files).
- A geography chapter: Beirut origins, Gulf championships, London, Southeast Asia, Japan, Europe, North America.
- A longevity chapter: 1993 UFO Radio badge through 2024 Stussy KL — 31 years of evidence.
- A credibility chapter: judgeships (DMC 2007, Red Bull 3Style), brand association (Red Bull, Pioneer DJ, Stussy), institutions (RBMA Bass Camp, SOAS London, Zouk KL).
- A maintainable archive: manifest-driven, add/replace/remove without redesign.

## 3. What Fragments is NOT

- Not a masonry gallery or Instagram wall.
- Not a celebrity wall: a famous person without a Skillz relationship earns no place.
- Not a chronology: sequence is thematic; dates appear only where supported.
- Not a sales page: no services, no rate hints, no marketing copy.
- Not a collection of "best photos": an imperfect photograph of a historic moment beats a pretty photo of nothing.
- Not the old build's "Living Archive / Digging Decades Vinyl Side A" concept: cryptic concept copy dies. FRAGMENTS, plainly editorial, documentary.

## 4. Core emotional objective

The visitor's arc: "Who is this guy?" → "Oh shit. He has actually been inside this." → respect, curiosity, trust. The emotion is delivered by evidence density — recognizable names, real places, dated moments — not by superlatives.

## 5. Business / credibility objective

A festival booker, conference organiser, brand, music executive, curator or journalist who lands here should leave with concrete reasons to book, invite, collaborate or commission: judge at DMC Dubai 2007 and Red Bull 3Style, relationship with Grandmaster Flash and Q-Bert, Wu-Tang and Bomb Squad circles, international reach (8 regions), institutional associations (Red Bull Music Academy, Pioneer DJ, Stussy, SOAS, Zouk), a 1993 origin. Nothing stated verbally; everything shown.

## 15. Caption / identity-label system (permanent requirement)

Every photograph with an important person shows the name as part of the visual language — museum/exhibition style:

- Name line: white, display/uppercase, small-tracking — e.g. `WITH GRANDMASTER FLASH` / `WITH DJ Q-BERT`
- Context line: muted, small — e.g. "DMC World DJ Championship, Dubai · judge" 
- Year/city: muted, tracked — only when supported
- Label placement: below or overlapping the plate edge — consistent, never on faces
- Tone: archival label, not social caption. No "@", no hashtags, no emoji, no exclamation
- Restraint: yellow accent used only for the chapter rule and structural markers; names stay white (the yellow-rule grammar established by Speaking/Turntablism)

## 16. Visual language

Black field, white typography, restrained yellow accent, documentary photography treated honestly, editorial hierarchy, cinematic spacing, asymmetry (Rhythm Before Symmetry), thin white/10 rules and borders (existing system), contact-sheet margins, museum-weight presentation. No gradients-as-decoration, no glassmorphism, no gimmicks.

## 21. Archive maintainability model

Manifest-driven ingestion (recommendation from the audit; unchanged):
- `source/` — immutable originals (current ARCHIVE contents stay untouched forever)
- `curated/` — web-ready derived assets (existing TURN_* naming convention; ffmpeg/PIL derivation scripts are the only writers)
- `manifest.json` — per asset: filename, subject, person(s), confidence, date, location, category/band, tier, caption lines, alt text, status (confirmed/unconfirmed)
- Website reads the manifest (same pattern as `src/lib/site.ts`): adding/replacing/removing an asset = file drop + manifest edit. No component changes ever needed for archive updates.
- Unconfirmed entries carry `status: unconfirmed` and render without dates or names until promoted.
